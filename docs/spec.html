Section 01

The normal ordering flow

Everyday orders. Confirmed rules: 48 hours' notice, maximum 3 per day, no minimum order value, delivery or pickup, no deposit, confirmed over WhatsApp.

Entry

Home → Order Now

The homepage offers two paths from the start: Order Now for everyday orders, and a clearly separate Plan an Event. Nobody should reach the wrong form and discover the rules only at the end.

Menu

Browse Main Courses, Side Dishes, Salads

Photo, name, price and any dietary flags per card. Unavailable dishes stay visible and marked.

Dish

Make the required choices

Full description, allergens, and the dish's choices — sauce, rice, side. Required groups must be answered before the dish can be added. Price updates live. Quantity, and a special-instructions box.

Cart

Saved in the browser

Survives closing the tab. Each line shows the choices made. Two trays of one dish with different sides are two lines, so the kitchen ticket is never ambiguous.

Step 1

Delivery or pickup

Chosen first, because it changes what's asked next. Pickup skips address and delivery fee entirely.

Step 2

Date and time

The calendar only offers dates at least 48 hours ahead that aren't blocked and haven't already taken 3 orders. A full or too-soon date is visibly unavailable, not merely rejected on submit.

Step 3

Serving setup

Returnable serving dishes, or disposable. Asked once per order. The policy text underneath is admin-editable — and is currently undefined.

Step 4

Details

Name, Egyptian mobile, optional email, and for delivery: area — which sets the fee — then address. Order notes.

Step 5

Review and submit

Full summary, fee, total, and the cancellation terms shown before the button — not buried. The date is re-checked at the moment of submitting in case the last slot went while they were typing.

Done

Confirmation, then WhatsApp

Order number, summary, date, setup choice, totals, and status New — with payment shown as unpaid, to be arranged. A prominent Confirm on WhatsApp button opens a chat with the summary pre-written. You get the same order as a WhatsApp alert.

Deliberate

The order is not final until you confirm it on WhatsApp — that's what you asked for, and the site says so plainly on the confirmation page. It keeps a human in the loop while the business is small, and it means a mistaken order costs you a message, not a day's cooking.

Section 02

The event ordering flow

You're right that this needs its own path. Different notice period, different cancellation terms, different questions. One important thing though: separate flow, not a separate system.

How I'd build it

Event orders live in the same orders table as normal ones, marked with a type. That way your revenue figures, dashboard, customer history and prep view include them automatically, and you never have to look in two places to answer "what's happening on Friday". The customer sees two separate journeys; you see one list you can filter.

Entry

Its own page explaining how event catering works — notice period, how confirmation happens, what you'll need from them.

Event type

Birthday · Engagement · Wedding · Other. Choosing Other reveals a free-text field, which is stored alongside the type rather than replacing it — so you can still count how many "other" events you get, and read what they were.

Submit

A request, not a booking

The event is submitted as a request with the date soft-held, and the customer is told clearly that you'll confirm. This is my recommendation and it's the safe answer to your capacity question — see below.

Your question: how should an event count against 3 orders a day?

You're right not to let one event equal one normal order. I looked at three ways to do it and I'd recommend the third.

ApproachHow it worksWhy not / why
Separate pools Normal orders capped at 3. Events capped separately, say 1 a day. Simple, but nothing stops an event and three normal orders landing on one day — which is the exact scenario you're worried about.
Weighted points Each day has a budget. A normal order costs 1. An event costs a number based on guest count — say 1 point per 25 guests. Accurate in principle, but it needs a guests-to-kitchen-load ratio that nobody knows yet. Guessing it would be exactly the kind of assumption you asked me not to make.
Manual confirmation Recommended Events are submitted as requests. The date is soft-held. When you confirm, you decide how much of that day it takes — by default, the whole day. No guesswork, and you cannot be oversold. You see the guest count and the dish list before committing. After a few months of real events you'll know the ratio, and we can switch on weighted points then.

The default matters here: I'd have a confirmed event block the day for normal orders unless you say otherwise, per event, with one tap. Safe by default, and you can always open the day back up. Tell me if you'd rather the default were the opposite.

What the slash marks turned out to mean

Your descriptions use "/" consistently to mean choose one, and that's how I've read every one of them. Eleven of the 21 main courses have a single required choice, usually the accompaniment. Two — Filleto meat and Roast beef — have two independent required choices: a sauce from four, and a side from two. Eight are fixed with no choices at all.

Three structural things worth knowing

Some things are both a dish and a choice. Basmati rice, coconut rice, noodles with vegetables and cream spinach all appear as side dishes you can order, and also as accompaniments inside main courses. Those are separate records — so changing the price of the Basmati rice side dish will not change any main course. That's correct, but it means two places to edit. I'd rather you knew now.

Serving sizes are optional. As you asked: not required for savoury dishes, available for desserts. The field exists on every product and simply stays empty where it doesn't apply.

Allergens are their own list, not a fixed set. Nuts and seafood to start, with dairy, gluten, egg, sesame and shellfish available, and room to add more. I can pre-tag from your own descriptions — almonds, walnuts, shrimps, salmon are all named in the text — but I'd want you to check every one before it goes live rather than trusting my reading of a PDF.

Still the number one blocker

How is each category sold and priced?

I asked last round and this is still open — it's the one answer I genuinely cannot work around. Per tray, per kilo, per person, per piece, or as a whole dish? Are Main Courses sold differently from Side Dishes and Salads? If by tray, what sizes are there? Until I know, I can't build quantity, the cart maths, or the price display — and I won't guess.

One thing to decide about the 48 hours

Is it 48 hours to the calendar date, or to the delivery slot? If someone orders at 9pm on Monday, a strict 48-hour rule makes Wednesday evening legal but Wednesday morning not — so the same date is half-available. The clean alternative is a daily cut-off: "order by 6pm and the day after tomorrow is available." Simpler to explain and simpler to see on a calendar. I need your call; I'd suggest the cut-off.

Conflict · needs your decision

The 20% charge cannot be collected at launch

There's no deposit and no online payment, so when someone cancels late there is no money in hand to deduct 20% from. As written, the policy is a statement rather than something the system can enforce. Three ways forward: state the policy, calculate the amount and chase it manually; or take a deposit on larger orders so there's something to deduct from; or leave it unenforced until online payments arrive. The system will compute and record the figure either way — what I need from you is which of those you actually intend.

Section 06

Admin and dashboard

Built for a phone in a kitchen. One order list covering both types, filterable.

Orders

One list showing normal and event orders together, each clearly badged. Filter by date, status, order type, fulfilment (delivery or pickup) and serving setup; search by order number, customer name or mobile. Opening an order shows everything: items with their chosen options and instructions, customer and address, date and slot, serving setup, totals, payment method and status, and — for events — event type, guest count and décor.

Statuses run New → Confirmed → Preparing → Ready → Out for Delivery → Delivered, plus Cancelled. Pickup orders skip Out for Delivery. Event requests start at Requested and only become Confirmed when you accept and decide the day's capacity. Payment status is tracked separately throughout, because a delivered order can still be unpaid.

Menu management

Add, edit and archive products; change prices; upload photos; manage categories; build option groups and their choices; set allergens; set serving sizes where relevant; mark anything unavailable. Archive rather than delete, so past orders keep making sense.

Settings

Delivery areas and fees, time slots, blocked dates, daily capacity, the two notice periods, cancellation windows and percentage, serving-setup policy text, and which payment methods are offered. All editable by you, none of it needing me.

Dashboard

Orders today, upcoming orders, revenue, order count, average order value, best-selling dishes and cancellations — with a date filter, and normal versus event split out. Written so cost and margin can be added once inventory exists.

Section 07

Kitchen prep view

In the MVP, as you asked. Pick a date, see what has to be cooked.

The screen totals every dish ordered for a chosen date across all orders — "3 × Roast Fakhda, 2 with mixed rice and 1 with grilled vegetables" — so the choices are aggregated too, not just the dish names. Because option choices change what's actually cooked, that breakdown is the part that makes it useful rather than decorative.

Normal and event orders are shown as separate blocks with their own totals, and a combined total underneath. Events carry their guest count and décor note. You can also see it per order rather than aggregated, for packing.

It respects your rules: only Confirmed and later orders appear by default, with unconfirmed requests shown separately so a pending event doesn't get cooked by mistake.

Later, cheaply

Once ingredients and recipes exist, this same screen becomes the shopping list — the dish totals multiply through to quantities of chicken, rice and sauce. It's built with that in mind, which costs nothing now.
