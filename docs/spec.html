How ordering works at The Diine

À la carte, 72 products across four categories, priced. Two customer journeys over one catalogue. This revision folds in your corrections and drops everything now settled.

72 products · 77 prices4 categories18 open items0 architecture blockers
Changed in this revision

The buffet question is closed

Resolved

The Full Buffet Menu is à la carte. Every dish is an individual product with its own price. There is no package builder, no "choose 3 mains", no per-person buffet — and no separate event menu. Both journeys use the same catalogue, the same cart and the same pricing. I've removed that uncertainty from this document entirely.

Also now settled: prices for all 77 rows, desserts as a fourth category, pickup counting toward the daily cap, a daily cut-off instead of rolling 48 hours, manual event confirmation with a per-event capacity choice, no customer self-cancellation, décor as a quote, and the payment structure. Each is written into the relevant section below.

Section 01

The normal ordering flow

Browse, choose, cart, checkout. 48 hours' notice via a daily cut-off, three orders a day, delivery or pickup.

Entry

Home → Order Now

Two clear paths from the homepage: Order Now and Plan an Event. Nobody discovers the rules only at the end.

Menu

Four categories

Main Courses, Side Dishes, Salads, Desserts. Photo, name, price and allergen flags per card. Unavailable dishes stay visible and marked.

Dish

Choose and configure

Required choices — sauce, rice, accompaniment — must be answered before the dish can be added. Where a dish has priced variants, choosing one sets the price. Quantity and special instructions.

Cart

Saved in the browser

Survives closing the tab. Each line carries its choices. The same dish with two different accompaniments is two lines, so the kitchen ticket is unambiguous.

Step 1

Delivery or pickup

Asked first. Pickup skips address and delivery fee entirely — but still counts toward the day's three orders.

Step 2

Date and time

The calendar offers only dates that clear the cut-off, aren't blocked, aren't taken by a confirmed event that blocked the day, and haven't already reached three orders. Unavailable dates look unavailable.

Step 3

Serving setup

Returnable serving dishes, or disposable ones the customer keeps. The policy text beneath it is admin-editable and currently empty.

Step 4

Details and payment method

Name, Egyptian mobile, optional email; for delivery, area then address. Payment method chosen here — nothing is charged online.

Step 5

Review and submit

Full summary, fee, total, cancellation terms shown before the button. The date is re-checked at the moment of submitting.

Done

Confirmation, then WhatsApp

Order number, summary, status New, payment unpaid, and a Confirm on WhatsApp button opening a chat to +20 11 23030107 with the order pre-written. The same order reaches you as a WhatsApp alert.

Section 02

The event ordering flow

Its own journey, the same catalogue underneath. Event details first, then the same menu and cart.

Entry

Plan an Event

Explains how event catering works before anything is asked: five days' notice, submitted as a request, confirmed by you.

Step 1

Event type

Birthday · Engagement · Wedding · Other. Choosing Other reveals a free-text field stored alongside the type, so "Other" stays countable and readable.

Step 2

Date, time and guests

Calendar enforces five days' minimum notice. Guest count is required.

Step 3

Table décor

Yes or No; Yes reveals a notes field. Recorded as a request to be quoted — it carries no price and adds nothing to the total.

Step 4

The food

The same menu, the same product pages, the same cart as a normal order. No separate event menu.

Step 5

Serving setup

Returnable or disposable, as with normal orders.

Step 6

Venue and details

Venue or delivery address, name, mobile, email.

Submit

A request, with the date soft-held

Status Requested. The customer is told plainly that you'll confirm. The date is held but not consumed until you accept.

You

Confirm, and choose the day

On confirming you pick Block Day — no further normal orders — or Keep Day Open, leaving the normal three-order cap running. Block Day is the default; you can change it on any event, and change your mind afterwards.

How the three kinds of choice work

Priced variants — Noodles, Spring Rolls, Tres Leches. One product, one photo, one page; choosing the variant sets the price. Admin types the actual price for each, not a difference.

Free required choices — the accompaniments on main courses. Must be answered, never change the price. If you later want to charge for one, the field is already there.

Add-ons — none in this menu. The structure supports them when you want them.

One instruction I had to reconcile

Your point 11 says to treat Noodles and Spring Rolls as one product with three priced choices; your point 12 says to leave that structure unresolved until prices are confirmed. Since point 11 confirms the prices, I've taken point 11 as the later instruction and built variants. Say the word if you meant the opposite.

Content gap · not an architecture blocker

Selling units are missing on almost everything

Roast Turkey at 4,500 — is that a whole turkey? Rokak at 900 — a tray, and how big? Mini Bites Baklava at 1,200 — how many pieces? I have not invented a single one. Every product has an empty, admin-editable selling unit field, and the menu simply shows the price without a unit until you fill them in. The build continues without them; the launch shouldn't.

Money is stored in piastres. 1,250 EGP is stored as 125000. It removes a whole class of rounding error from totals, and you'll never see it — you type pounds.

Cancellation, precisely

Customers cannot cancel themselves — they contact you, and you cancel from the dashboard. The system records the time, who did it, the reason you type, whether it fell inside or outside the free window, and the 20% figure calculated from the order total. Collection is yours to handle; nothing is taken automatically, and no deposit is introduced to enforce it.

Section 06

Payment

Three methods in the structure from day one. None of them charge a card online at launch.

Cash
On delivery, or on collection for pickup orders. Available now.
InstaPay
Customer transfers and tells you. Available now — verification is a separate step from confirming the order, so you can accept an order and separately mark the money as actually received.
Card
In the structure, switched off. Added later through a payment gateway. No card details ever touch the system — the gateway handles them, which is where they belong.
Order status
New · Confirmed · Preparing · Ready · Out for Delivery · Delivered · Cancelled. Events add Requested at the front; pickup skips Out for Delivery.
Payment status
Tracked entirely separately: unpaid · awaiting verification · paid · refunded. A delivered order can be unpaid; a paid order can still be cooking.
Section 07

Admin and dashboard

Orders

One list covering both types, each badged. Filter by date, status, type, fulfilment and payment status; search by order number, name or mobile. An order shows items with their variants, choices and instructions, the customer, the address or venue, date and slot, serving setup, totals, payment method and status — and for events, the type, guest count and décor request.

Menu management

Add, edit and archive products; edit prices and variant prices; upload photos; manage the four categories; build required-choice groups; set allergens; set serving sizes and selling units; mark anything unavailable. Archive rather than delete, so past orders still read correctly.

Settings

Delivery areas and fees, time slots, working days, blocked dates, daily capacity, cut-off time, both notice periods, cancellation windows and percentage, the serving-setup policy text, allergen list, and which payment methods are on. All yours to change.

Dashboard

Orders today, upcoming, revenue, order count, average order value, best sellers, cancellations — date-filtered, with normal and event split out.

Section 08

Kitchen prep view

In the MVP. Pick a date, see what must be cooked.

Totals every dish for a date across all orders, aggregating the choices too — "3 × Roast Fakhda: 2 mixed rice, 1 grilled vegetables" — because the choices change what's actually cooked. Normal and event orders appear as separate blocks with their own totals plus a combined figure; events carry guest count and décor note.

Unconfirmed event requests are excluded from the confirmed totals and shown separately, so nothing pending gets cooked by mistake.
